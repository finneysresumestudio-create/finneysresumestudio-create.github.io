The Story Builder Profile

If this information is already in your resume, great! Otherwise, review the prompts for your stage and include what's relevant in the body of your email. There is no need to bake it into your current resume.

Many of these questions remain relevant in college and early career and will eventually fall off your resume as you build more experience, but for now, it's all a part of your story.

Education & Activities

  • High school name and GPA (if you'd like to include it)
  • Clubs, sports, and activities (high school and college)
  • If still in college: your major, minor, current year, and expected graduation year

Recognition & Leadership

  • Awards, honors, academic distinctions, and recognition
  • Leadership roles (e.g., captain, president, officer)
  • RAPs, advisory boards, or university committees

A few focused questions help us position what you've done and where you're headed.

Direction

  • What type of job are you hoping to land?
  • Any industries, causes, or types of organizations you're drawn to?

Impression

  • What do you want people to feel or think when they read your resume or LinkedIn?

For senior leaders, the story is a cohesive narrative of impact, leadership, and vision. Share what's relevant across these areas.

Career Arc

  • Briefly describe your career arc: where you started, how you've grown, and where you are now
  • Industries you've worked in
  • Types of organizations (startup, Fortune 500, nonprofit, government, etc.)

Leadership & Scope

  • How would you describe your leadership philosophy or style?
  • Your most significant leadership accomplishments
  • Team and organizational scope: how large of a team have you led?
  • Have you led through major change, such as restructuring, mergers, turnarounds, or rapid growth?

P&L & Budget Ownership

  • Have you held P&L responsibility? If so, describe the scope
  • The largest budget you've managed (ranges are fine)
  • Revenue or cost impact you've driven, and key financial outcomes

Board & Advisory

  • Current or past board memberships
  • Speaking engagements, published work, or thought leadership

Goals & Target Role

  • What role are you targeting (title, function, level)?
  • Geography: are you open to relocation or remote?
  • What do you want your resume or LinkedIn to convey that it doesn't currently?
  • Any additional information you believe is useful

Cover letters aren't always required, and they're not always read, but they can be valuable in the right situations. Think of them as a strategic tool rather than a mandatory step.
